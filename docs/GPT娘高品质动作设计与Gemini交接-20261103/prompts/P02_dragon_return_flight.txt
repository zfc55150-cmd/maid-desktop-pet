ACTION P02 — THE WHITE DRAGON'S RETURNING TURN / 白龙回翔. Target 12 seconds. This is the physical movement showcase, not the existing bent-knee hovering action.

Locked camera. Character moves inside the video canvas; no real desktop-window travel is required. Provide a documented wider safe canvas if necessary. Maximum foot clearance approximately 0.25H above the neutral baseline; horizontal excursion roughly plus/minus 0.22H. No per-frame resizing. Preserve original small wing size, dress coverage, body proportions and connected curled tail.

0–1.3 s: her gaze finds the planned turn direction. Chin lowers slightly, one foot retreats half a shoe length, knees bend, weight loads into the support leg. Hands slightly clear hanging sleeve/skirt edges for motion without exposing more of her body.
1.3–2.4 s: the support foot pushes off. Hips and chest lift first; her small wings make one clear down-and-back beat immediately afterward. Hair tips and skirt panels respond late. Legs stay close and the dress maintains its existing coverage.
2.4–5.2 s: while following a small low aerial arc, she makes ONE full turn around her body's upright axis. This is an elegant upright rotating turn, NOT a somersault. Front, side, back and side connect structurally using the turnaround reference. Shoulder/hip rotation drives the movement; wing orientation, tail, separate hair masses and skirt panels react independently. Absolutely no rotating the whole flat cutout as an image.
5.2–6.8 s: as she reaches a three-quarter-front orientation, the chest begins braking first. Small wings adjust angle. The original tail coil swings outward from its connected root as a counterbalance, then recoils slightly later, visibly helping arrest rotation. Her eyes find the landing point before her feet. No giant wings, large vortex, full-screen streaks, or fog to disguise the mechanics.
6.8–8.2 s: one forefoot lands first; ankle, knee and hip absorb the descent. The second foot follows and establishes balance. Arms assist. Skirt panels and hair finish descending after the body has become stable. No skating feet, floating again after impact, or rigid full-body bobbing.
8.2–9.8 s: she glances at the viewer with quiet pride, notices one side lock of hair near her eye, briefly pauses, then tidies it with one hand. She has completed a skilled maneuver; do not turn this into a pratfall or a big embarrassed laugh.
9.8–12 s: hand lowers, small wings fold, tail returns to its original side/coil, shoulders extend naturally. Hair, skirt and tassels settle at different times. End in C-compatible neutral with the last half-second stable.

Mandatory: visible leg-powered takeoff, true spatial body rotation, wing-and-tail braking, weight-bearing landing, then the small hair-tidying character beat. Use a simple rig/blocking pass and about twelve designed key poses before final generation/paint if required. If a single image-to-video pass cannot perform the full turn while preserving identity, report that and use a controlled layered/keyframe workflow; do not silently downgrade to hovering or slow side-to-side sway.
