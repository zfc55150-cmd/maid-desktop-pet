ACTION P05 — MENDING A BROKEN MOON / 裂月金缮. Target 14 seconds. A tactile, precise craft performance, not an instant repair spell.

Prop: a matte dark-violet-gray crescent porcelain ornament, complete height about 0.27H, broken into exactly THREE persistent pieces. LEFT palm supports the lower main piece. The other two pieces begin visibly separated beside it. RIGHT hand works with one short gold-repair applicator. Keep stable fragment topology and seam location. The warm gold seam is thin but readable against dark porcelain at small size. Do not turn it into a bright rope, rune circle or glowing fog.

0–2 s: her LEFT palm presents the damaged lower crescent. Two matching fragments appear nearby. She looks closely, brows focus briefly, and her RIGHT index finger traces the intended edge alignment in the air. She is solving a craft problem, not sadly holding a broken object.
2–5 s: RIGHT hand first rotates the larger fragment into the correct orientation, then gently seats it against the palm-supported main piece. Show edge alignment BEFORE contact. LEFT wrist adjusts its support; her gaze stays on the join and torso leans closer a little.
5–8 s: a short applicator appears in the RIGHT hand. Its tip touches and moves along the first seam from bottom to top. A fine continuous gold line forms immediately behind the actual contact. Wrist guides the stroke; no broad arm flourish. The third small fragment then approaches along its proper mating direction and is placed in alignment.
8–10 s: she applies gold along the second seam, lifts the tool and lightly presses a clean porcelain edge with her fingertip to confirm the fit. Her brow relaxes. Gold is initially restrained and matte-bright; it does not flash into a magic symbol.
10–11.8 s: LEFT palm presents the complete crescent, slowly turning the wrist about 20 degrees. One consistent light direction produces a restrained moving highlight on the fixed gold seams while the matte porcelain remains tactile. RIGHT open hand frames the object without hiding it. Her eyes lift from the result to the viewer; a small satisfied smile appears.
11.8–14 s: the tool exits first. The ornament lowers between both palms toward the waist and exits locally; hands return to neutral. Head/shoulders stabilize, then sleeves and hair. End C-compatible with the final half-second stable.

Mandatory: all three pieces actually mate; two visible contact-driven gold strokes; a genuine reveal through wrist rotation and material response; reserved satisfaction. Reject instant broken-to-whole morphing, fragments merging before alignment, seams changing places, a shiny finished object replacing all craft, fused hands, or a static ornament with generic sparkles. Stable controlled prop rendering and painted 2D integration are welcome.
